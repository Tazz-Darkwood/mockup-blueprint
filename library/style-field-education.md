# Education

A field guide. Field guides are the third shelf of the style stack, beside purpose and feel: they record what visitors to one kind of business expect to find and how the good sites in that trade present it. Stack this with a feel guide, usually `style-feel-professional.md`. The site's own guide wins over it, and the general guide's accessibility minimums still hold.

## Who visits, and what they want

Two audiences at once, and they are not the same person: the **learner** and whoever **pays or decides**, usually a parent. Parents are judging safety, competence and value. Learners are judging whether they will be talked down to. Good education sites speak to both and say which part is for whom.

## The detail map for an education site

| Part of the page | Level | What it holds | Seen on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top of the first page | Styled | A photograph of learners or the teacher, one short line, and the main task | 11 of 12 |
| The main task | Styled, in the first or second screen | For an institution, finding a course; for a service, booking a first conversation | Course finder on 3 of 3 universities studied closely; a first-step button on 5 of 6 tutoring sites |
| Routes by audience | Quiet | "I am a student / a parent / a teacher" where the audiences differ | Khan Academy; university menus |
| Outcomes | Styled | Results, rankings, years, numbers helped | 10 of 12 within two screens |
| How it works | Quiet, three steps | What happens first, next and after | 4 of 6 tutoring sites |
| People | Styled | Real learners and real teachers, photographed | 10 of 12 |
| Colour | One strong colour on white | Universities sober; tutoring brighter and rounder | 10 of 12 |
| Type | One or two families; a serif for headings is common in higher education | | 3 of 4 universities |

## Use it properly

1. In the brief, name both audiences and say which one each section is written for.
2. Decide the main task and put it in the first screen.
3. Collect the outcomes that are true and checkable before designing where they go.

## Notes

### Speak to the learner and the parent, and say which is which
- Status: draft
- Source: study. Khan Academy opens by asking who the visitor is (student, family, teacher, school). Three of the six tutoring sites address parents by name; Mathnasium's proof line is "trusted by over 1,000,000 parents".
- Rule: sections written for the parent say so ("questions parents ask"), and the learner is never spoken about as if absent.

### The main task is in the first screen
- Status: draft
- Source: study. Imperial puts "Find a course" in a bar across the top of its home page; Hull and SOAS have a course search within two screens; five of six tutoring sites put one first-step button in the first screen ("Book your free assessment", "Find a center near you", "Get started now").
- Rule: one obvious next step at the top, named for what happens: "book a first conversation", not "learn more".

### Show the people learning
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 10 of 12 opened on a photograph of students or a teacher; none of the tutoring sites led with an abstract image.
- Rule: real photographs of the teacher, and of learners only with permission. Any photograph that shows a child needs written permission from a parent first.

### Outcomes as numbers, honestly
- Status: draft
- Source: study. "4.9 average session rating", "3 million children helped", "45+ years", league-table positions. One trade article reports that specific results persuade more than lists of qualifications.
- Rule: a few true, specific figures near the top. Never a promised grade or score. If there are no numbers yet, lead with qualifications and say less.

### Explain what happens, in three steps
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 4 of 6 tutoring sites; three drew it as a numbered row of three.
- Rule: first contact, first session, what continues. One sentence each.

### On a phone, the face and the first step share the first screen
- Status: draft
- Source: the twelve sites opened at phone width. A photograph of people was in the first screen on 8 of 12, and a heading with one action on 10 of 12. They split on order: photograph first on Imperial, SOAS and Mathnasium; heading and button first on Hull and Kumon. Three of the six tutoring companies keep a way to call in the header.
- Rule: on a phone the first screen holds the heading, the one first step and a face. If the photograph would push the heading out of the first screen, make the photograph smaller, not later. General phone rules are in `style-mobile.md`.

## Tutoring

What follows is narrower: sites for tutors and tutoring services.

**A caution about the evidence.** The six tutoring sites studied are all large companies and marketplaces. No single tutor's own site was found to study. So the notes below say what the companies do, what two trade articles advise, and where one person should deliberately do something different.

### The heading names the subject, the level and the place
- Status: draft
- Source: trade article (Wepage): a heading such as "Austin – High School Math Tutor"; "specific subject lists rather than broad terms". Study: the company sites lead with the service in plain words.
- Rule: subject, level, and where or how (a town, or online) in the first line a visitor reads.

### The tutor's own face and qualifications come first
- Status: draft
- Source: Stanford credibility guidelines 3 and 4 (show the expertise; show the real people). Both trade articles: a warm professional photograph; degrees, certification and years of teaching stated.
- Rule: a real photograph of the tutor in the first screen, with their degree, their teaching experience and how long they have tutored. For anyone meeting children in person, say whether there is a current background check, and only if there is one.

### Say what it costs
- Status: draft
- Source: Nielsen Norman Group on up-front disclosure; both trade articles advise showing rates or a range. Study: only 1 of 6 companies showed a price on the page studied.
- Rule: a single tutor shows their rates. The companies mostly do not; that is a reason to differ from them, not to copy them.

### Offer a small first step
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 5 of 6 offered something free or low-risk first: a free assessment, a free consultation, a first hour refunded if the match is wrong.
- Rule: if the tutor offers a short first conversation, make that the main button. If they do not, do not invent one.

### Reviews are specific, named and real
- Status: draft
- Source: study (Mathnasium: "Pam M., parent"; Wyzant shows the number of lessons behind each review). Trade article: specific outcomes over vague praise.
- Rule: each review names who wrote it, their role (parent or student) and when. No invented or paraphrased reviews, and no review is allowed to imply a guaranteed result.

### Make contact effortless, by more than one route
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 5 of 6 kept a phone number in the header. Stanford guideline 5. Trade articles: several contact methods and a stated reply time.
- Rule: email and at least one other way, visible on every screen on a phone, with the reply time the tutor will actually keep.

### One tutor is not a company
- Status: draft
- Source: judgement, from `style-feel-professional.md` and the general guide.
- Rule: do not copy the companies' apparatus: no chat widget, no promotional banner, no "guarantee", no numbers that imply scale. One tutor's strengths are being a named, qualified person a parent can speak to. The page should feel like that.

## Applied so far

- **AP Biology tutor site, 2026-10-03** (a restyle of an existing mockup; the owner agreed the result the same day: "way better, cleaner for sure". One site so far; a second is needed before any note here is approved). Three things came from this guide: the heading was reworded to name the subject, the level and the place; the second button at the top became a plain link, leaving one first step; and the section about the tutor moved up to follow the top directly. One rule could not be met from what the mockup had: nothing on the page is written to the student, so that became a question for the tutor. On a phone the portrait first fell below the first screen; it was then made a small picture beside the tutor's name, above the heading (see "On a phone" below).

## Not covered yet

- No single tutor's site has been studied.
- Schools for younger children, which are usually warmer and more playful.
- Course pages and other inner pages; only home pages were studied.

## How these notes get approved

A note here is approved when it has held on two education sites built with this guide and the skill's owner has agreed with the result on both.

## Questions it raises

- Who decides and who learns, and is the page speaking to both?
- What is the one first step?
- Which outcomes are true and can be shown?
- Will rates be public?
- Is there a current background check?
