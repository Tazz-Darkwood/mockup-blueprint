# Professional

A feel guide. It is stacked on `style-guide.md` (the general guide) when the site's job is to be believed. It gives way to a field guide (`style-field-...`) where that knows the trade better, and to the site's own guide above all. The general guide's accessibility minimums still hold.

**This guide is thin on purpose.** It holds only what is true of professional sites in any field, and so far only one field, education, has been studied. It will fill out as more fields are.

Professional is not the same as corporate. A firm of two hundred and a single tutor can both be professional; they should not look alike. The general guide's rule "who is behind the site sets how polished it is" still applies: one person's professional site shows the person, speaks as "I", and is plain about what they can and cannot do.

## What changes from an artistic site

On an artistic site the detail goes into one signature piece and the repeated items. On a professional site **the detail goes into the evidence**: who is behind it, what qualifies them, what results they can show, what it costs and how to reach them. Everything else is quiet and orderly, so the evidence is what the eye lands on.

## The detail map for a professional site

Levels are the three from the general guide: signature, styled, quiet.

| Part of the page | Level | What it holds | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top of the first page | Styled, not signature | In plain words, what is offered and to whom; a photograph of real people; one next step | 11 of 12 sites studied |
| Proof: numbers, credentials, results | Styled, and near the top | A few checkable facts set large | All 6 tutoring sites and 4 of 6 education sites, within two screens |
| What others say | Styled | Named reviews or outside ratings | Research: outside validation is trusted more than the site's own claims |
| Navigation | Quiet, conventional, short | A handful of plainly named items | Research; study |
| Repeated items (services, courses, people) | Quiet, identical | Same layout for each, in an orderly grid | Study |
| Contact | Quiet, and impossible to miss | More than one way, with what to expect | Research |
| Colour | One institutional colour on a white or near-white ground | | 10 of 12 |
| Type | One or two sober families; headings about three times body size at most | | Study: 50 to 65px against 16 to 20px on the education sites |
| Decoration | None without a job | | Research: restraint |

## Use it properly

1. In the brief, say who is behind the site and what a visitor must believe before they will act.
2. List the evidence available: credentials, years, numbers, named reviews, outside ratings, photographs of the real people. If there is little, say so; the design cannot invent it.
3. Put the offer, a real face and the strongest piece of evidence in the first screen.
4. Before showing the mockup, read every claim on the page and ask "how would a visitor check this?"

## Notes

### Say plainly what is offered, to whom, in the first screen
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 11 of 12. Tutoring sites led with the result or the service ("Academic Tutoring & Homework Help", "Go from Basics to Breakthroughs"); university sites with one line and a way to find a course.
- Rule: the heading names the service and who it is for in ordinary words. No slogan that could belong to any business.

### Show the real people
- Status: draft
- Source: Stanford credibility guideline 4 ("show that honest and trustworthy people stand behind your site... convey their trustworthiness through images or text") and guideline 2 (show there is a real organisation, for example with an address). Study: 10 of 12 opened on a photograph of people.
- Rule: photographs of the actual people, named, with what qualifies them. Never stock photographs standing in for staff or clients.

### Put the evidence where the eye lands
- Status: draft
- Source: Stanford guideline 3 ("highlight the expertise in your organization"). Study: every tutoring site showed figures within two screens (ratings, numbers helped, years), and four of the six education sites showed rankings or outcomes.
- Rule: two to four facts a visitor could check, set larger than body text, near the top. Qualifications before adjectives.

### Let others vouch
- Status: draft
- Source: Nielsen Norman Group ("people trust testimonials from external sites more than those listed on the website itself"); Stanford guideline 1 (make it easy to verify, link to the evidence).
- Rule: where an outside rating, listing or press mention exists, show it and link to it. Reviews on the site carry a name, a role and a date.

### Disclose up front
- Status: draft
- Source: Nielsen Norman Group ("people appreciate when sites are upfront with all information that relates to the customer experience"; users abandoned sites that hid prices or demanded sign-up first).
- Rule: prices or a price range, terms, and what happens next are on the page, before any form.

### Contact is easy and says what to expect
- Status: draft
- Source: Stanford guideline 5 ("making your contact information clear: phone number, physical address, and email address"). Study: 5 of 6 tutoring sites kept a phone number in the header.
- Rule: more than one way to get in touch, reachable from every screen, with a stated reply time the business will keep.

### Restraint
- Status: draft
- Source: Stanford guideline 9 ("use restraint with any promotional content... avoid pop-up ads") and guideline 6 (the design should match the site's purpose). Study: the university sites used one strong colour on white and almost no ornament.
- Rule: one accent colour, an orderly grid, generous space, nothing that moves by itself, no pop-ups, no countdowns. Ornament that has no job is removed.

### No errors, and nothing stale
- Status: draft
- Source: Stanford guideline 10 ("typographical errors and broken links hurt a site's credibility more than most people imagine") and guideline 8 (show the content is current). Nielsen Norman Group: one spelling mistake on a home page damaged trust.
- Rule: every link works, every word is checked, and nothing on the page can go out of date without someone noticing. A dated line that will not be kept current is left out.

### Professional is not corporate
- Status: draft
- Source: judgement, from the Greenfire Herbs critique and the general guide's rule on who is behind a site.
- Rule: match the polish to the size of the business. One practitioner writes as "I", shows their own face, and does not borrow the look of a large company: no "our team", no implied scale, no chat widget.

## Applied so far

- **AP Biology tutor site, 2026-10-03** (a restyle of an existing mockup; the owner agreed the result the same day: "way better, cleaner for sure". One site so far; a second is needed before any note here is approved). The existing mockup already had nearly everything this guide asks for in content: a portrait, three facts, rates, named reviews, several ways to get in touch. What the guide changed was emphasis: the facts and qualifications went from small type and labels to large type near the top, and twenty rounded boxes became ruled rows, so that the evidence is what stands out. Lesson so far: on a professional site the guide's main work is deciding what is loud and what is quiet, not adding things.
