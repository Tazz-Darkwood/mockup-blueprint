# Almanac plate

For a stargazing society, a tide table, a garden calendar or anything ruled by the hour and the sky. Every word sits on a sheet of printed paper; behind the sheets, the sky is the visitor's own, now. Calm, exact and welcoming.

## What makes it

- The sky behind the page is the visitor's own sky at this moment: sun, moon, stars and the dusk glow follow their clock, the time is set large on the first sheet, and a control (and `?t=HH:MM`) lets anyone try another hour.
- The page walks down a hill, band by band: sky, the hut, the lamp-lit track, the earth. The background pick (one-long-scene) suits playful and dramatic sites; it is here on purpose and kept spare: flat land shapes, one subject per band drawn in the half the sheet leaves empty, and each edge the land's own contour traced in a hairline.
- Words only ever sit on paper sheets whose colour never changes with the clock, so contrast is measured once and holds at every hour; at night the sheets read like a chart under a torch.
- Torch red for actions, because a dim red light keeps the eye used to the dark; the lamps on the track are a different, orange red and are only decoration.

## Not covered yet

- Sunrise and sunset are fixed at 06:00 and 18:00, not the real times for the place.
- The plates are drawn in hairline, not engraved; true engraving is a step further than the mockup went.
- On a phone the sky shows only round the sheet's edges, and the scene's subjects appear only from 62rem.
- Whether a reduced-motion visitor should see the minute-by-minute change of sky (it was kept, as it is not motion).
