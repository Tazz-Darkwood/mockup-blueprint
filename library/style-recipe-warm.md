# Warm

For a site that belongs to a group of people: a club, a volunteer group, a charity, a community project. What the twelve studied sites shared is that somebody's hand is visible. This recipe sets every part to that; what it adds is the group's own voice and its own things.

## What makes it

- The biggest words are a slogan someone in the group would say out loud, the kind painted on a banner. What the page is for goes in the title and a smaller line.
- The page is made of one material from the group's real life. Ask what it would pin to a wall and what that is made of (a poster, a sign-up sheet, felt and pins, chalk), and make the ground, the edges and the repeated item from it.
- The repeated item (an event, a project, a group) is drawn as the object it is off the screen: a notice on a board, a ticket, a seed packet. Each may have its own colour and its own small drawing.
- Counts and states are shown the way the group would show them by hand: names on a sheet, places ticked off, a FULL stamp. The number is always written in words too.

## Notes from the owner

- On the first volunteer page, made with no feel guide: "very plain and used the same format a lot of websites do, so it looked like I made it with a cheap make-your-own-website tool."
- On the same page made warm: "looks great now, very styled, I love it."
- On a shop where warm was stacked second and the page came out dark anyway: "B just kinda looks like a worse version of A ... nothing really feels warm about B".
- On warm leading with a dark guide after it: it should be "a warm site with a hint of the sorcery", where the guides "flavor each other so it fills everything but with warm things".

## Used on

A volunteer sign-up page; a repair café (warm with a detailed ground and busy density; see the repair-cafe recipe); the warm half of a shop for a fair-stall group.

## Not covered yet

- When warm is not the lead: the old guide kept the people (names, their voice, one of their own things) and one bold colour of warm's kind, and gave up the bright page and the top. No recipe says how to stack yet.
- Tilts: the old guide asked for at least one thing tilted one to three degrees, the paper turned and the words kept level. The frames and shapes picks give cut and torn edges, not the tilt.
- Fields, buttons, errors and long text stay plain and straight, in the reading face, with no decoration over them. The parts allow it; no part says it as one rule for this look.
- Where each repeated item's colour and drawing come from on the real site (somebody has to pick one when an event is added).
- Phones beyond the volunteer page; inner pages and forms.
- A single maker's site needs the maker's own name and voice too; a site that must look official first (admissions, a clinic) should use the professional recipe and borrow at most the photograph of real people.
