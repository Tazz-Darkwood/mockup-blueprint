# Artistic

A feel guide. It is stacked on `style-guide.md` (the general guide) when a site should feel artistic, and it gives way to the site's own guide wherever that says something more specific. The general guide's accessibility minimums (contrast, text size, tap target size) still hold.

This guide does not describe a look. Seven acclaimed artistic sites were studied and they look nothing alike: a museum under a full-screen painting, a gallery made of bordered boxes and circles, a studio in white type on black. What they share is **where the detailed styling goes and how much of it there is**. That is what this guide records. What the detail actually is (a brush circle, a block of yellow, a chrome letterform) is decided per site and written in that site's guide.

## Levels of detail

Every part of a page gets one of three levels:

- **Signature.** One custom piece, unique to this site, that a visitor would describe to someone else. It takes real design effort and there is only one, or very few.
- **Styled.** A designed treatment that echoes the signature in material, colour or shape. More than tokens, less than a one-off.
- **Quiet.** Plain. Tokens only, no decoration. It should not draw the eye.

## The detail map for an artistic site

| Part of the page | Level | Seen on |
|---|---|---|
| Top of the first page | Signature | 7 of 7 |
| The site's name | Signature or styled: oversized, custom, or part of the top | 6 of 7 |
| Navigation | Quiet | 7 of 7 |
| The main action | Quiet in shape, accent in colour, and only one | 6 of 7 |
| The repeated content item (a work, an exhibition, a poem) | Styled | 7 of 7 |
| Typeface | One family with character carrying the whole site | 5 of 7 |
| Colour | A neutral base; colour comes from the content, or from one bold flat block | 7 of 7 |
| Layout | Leaves the centred column: runs to the edge, or varies in size | 5 of 7 |
| Top of the other pages | Styled: a lighter echo of the signature | Not studied; from Seventeen |
| Forms, fields, menus, dialogs, footer | Quiet | Little evidence; assumed |

## Use it properly

1. In the design brief, name the signature piece in one sentence before drawing anything.
2. Go down the detail map and decide, for this site, what each row will be. Write the answers in the site's own guide.
3. Spend the effort in that order: the top first, then the repeated item, then stop. Everything else is quiet.
4. Before showing the mockup, check each row of the map against the page. A row that is quieter or louder than the map says needs a reason.

## Notes

### The top of the first page is the signature piece
- Status: draft
- Source: study of seven sites, 7 of 7. Rijksmuseum: a full-screen painting under a name set the width of the window. Baltic: a full-screen exhibition photograph. Van Gogh Museum: a block of yellow taken from the painting beside it. Nottingham Contemporary: the whole first screen is a collage of boxes and circles. Studio Size: one line of very large type on black.
- Rule: the first screen holds one strong idea that fills most of it. It is not a heading, a paragraph and a button in the middle of the page.
- Also seen on: Seventeen, where the brush circle at the top was the part that worked.

### The signature comes from the subject
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 7 of 7 took it from what the site is about: the artwork itself, the institution's name, or the studio's own work. On Seventeen, which has only text, it had to be made.
- Rule: build the signature from the site's own material. If the site has images of its subject, the best one is the signature. If it has none, the signature has to be drawn, lettered or composed for it, and the brief should say which.

### The repeated item gets a designed frame
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 7 of 7. Exhibition tiles that run to the edge, image blocks beside flat colour blocks, bordered boxes with tags. None used a plain white card with rounded corners and a shadow. Confirmed on Seventeen: poems as a plain list read as empty, and the version that framed each poem as a small print was the one that worked.
- Rule: whatever the page repeats (a poem, a product, a work) is styled as an object in the site's material. Design one item until it could stand alone as a picture, then repeat it.

### Navigation and controls stay quiet
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 7 of 7 for navigation (small plain text, or one menu button), 6 of 7 for a single plain main button.
- Rule: no decoration on navigation, buttons, fields, menus or dialogs. Their job is to disappear next to the signature. One main action, in the accent colour, in a plain shape.
- Careful: four of the seven set navigation text at 12px or smaller. Do not copy that. The general guide's minimum sizes hold.

### One typeface with character, pushed in size
- Status: draft
- Source: study. 5 of 7 used a single family for everything, and it was a distinctive one (a custom museum face, Caslon, a heavy grotesque). The largest text was 3 to 5 times the body size on 4 of 7 (60 against 16, 82 against 17, 108 against 20).
- Rule: choose one family with a recognisable character and let size do the work. On the top of the first page, the largest text is at least three times the body size. A second family is allowed by the general guide but is not what makes a site artistic.

### Colour comes from the content
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 7 of 7 kept a neutral base (black, white, grey, cream). The published review of these sites notes the Van Gogh Museum changing colour with each painting and the Nordic Museum giving each exhibition a palette taken from its own material.
- Rule: keep the base neutral and take the colour from the work being shown, or commit to one bold flat colour. Do not add a decorative palette that has nothing to do with the subject.

### Leave the centred column
- Status: draft
- Source: study, 5 of 7 ran sections to the edge of the window or varied the size of items instead of repeating equal ones.
- Rule: at least one thing on the page breaks the central column: an image that runs to the edge, an item larger than its neighbours, a staggered row. Reading text still keeps its maximum line length.

### A text-only site has to make its art
- Status: draft
- Source: judgement, from Seventeen. Every studied site had images of its subject to show; a site of plain text has none.
- Rule: when the content is words, plan the artwork as part of the design: a made signature piece and a made element for each repeated item. In the blueprint, say where each item's artwork comes from in the real site, because the mockup's will be placeholders.

### Decoration is hidden from assistive technology
- Status: draft
- Source: judgement; applied on Seventeen.
- Rule: artwork that carries no information is marked so screen readers skip it, and nothing a person needs is only in the artwork.

## When this guide is not the lead

Another feel guide leads, so the page's colour, what fills its top and its main material are that guide's (see "When guides are stacked" in `style-guide.md`). Artistic flavours it, in the lead's terms:

- **Keep:** the work itself shown large and framed as work, one family of type with character, and a layout that leaves the centred column somewhere.
- **Give up:** the neutral base and the signature at the top: the lead decides those.
- Judgement: not yet tried as a second guide.

## What this guide does not give you

Artistic is not the same as handmade. The sites this guide was built from are museums and design studios: expressive, and also polished. Turning this guide up makes a site bolder and more gallery-like. It does not make it warmer or more personal. When the business is one person's craft, the site also needs the maker's own hand and voice, and that is a different quality (see "Who is behind the site sets how polished it is" in the general guide). On Greenfire Herbs it was added as tape, pen lines, handwriting and a first-person voice, recorded in that site's own guide. If a second site needs the same, it should become a feel guide of its own, built from a study of small makers' sites.

## Not covered yet

- Motion. The published review credits several of these sites' effect to animation on load and on scroll, which a screenshot cannot show. Nothing here is about motion.
- The inner pages. Only home pages were studied.

## How these notes get approved

A note here is approved when it has held on two different sites built with this guide and the skill's owner has agreed with the result on both. One site is not enough: it cannot show whether the rule is about artistic sites or only about that one.

## Questions it raises
